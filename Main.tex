\section{Introduction}

Ground-based astronomical spectra are affected by absorption in the Earth’s atmosphere. These telluric absorption features contaminate the observed spectrum and can interfere with the detection and analysis of astrophysical spectral lines, particularly when stellar and atmospheric features overlap in wavelength. Accurate modelling and correction of telluric absorption is therefore an essential step in the reduction and interpretation of many spectroscopic observations.

\section{PyMolFit framework}

\pymolfit implements the model-based telluric-correction approach used by \Molecfit\ \citep{MOLECFIT1,MOLECFIT2} in Python, with assistance from AI coding tools during development. Parameter fitting, calculation of atmospheric transmission over the full spectral range, and application of the telluric correction are combined into a single function, \texttt{correct()}. This function accepts spectrum files, loaded spectrum objects, or wavelength--flux arrays and returns the corrected spectrum together with the fitted transmission, model parameters, masks, and diagnostic information.

PyMolFit automatically divides the spectrum into smaller processing segments while preserving spectral-order and detector-group boundaries. Fitting regions can be selected automatically or defined and edited through an interactive selector. When a theoretical stellar spectrum is supplied, PyMolFit uses it to identify stellar features and exclude the corresponding wavelength regions from telluric parameter estimation. These exclusions affect only the fit: atmospheric transmission is still calculated in the excluded regions when applying the correction.

The atmospheric modelling follows established physical methods rather than introducing a new formalism. However, whereas \Molecfit uses \LBLRTM for its radiative-transfer calculations \citep{MOLECFIT1}, PyMolFit evaluates atmospheric absorption through its own numerical implementation, without executing either program. Differences in numerical implementation and optimisation can therefore produce different transmission spectra and fitted parameters. These differences are assessed in Sect.~\ref{sec:validation}.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Atmospheric profile and absorption calculation}

PyMolFit follows the atmospheric-profile construction used by \Molecfit\ \citep{MOLECFIT1}, combining a MIPAS reference atmosphere \citep{MIPAS_PROFILE} with GDAS pressure, temperature, and water-vapour profiles \citep{GDAS}. Conditions at the observation time are estimated from a time-weighted average of the bracketing GDAS profiles. When these are unavailable, PyMolFit uses the same two-month average profiles as Molecfit. Available local weather measurements adjust the lowest layers. The molecular number density at altitude \(h\) is
\begin{equation} n_s(h) = x_s(h)\,\frac{P(h)}{k_{\mathrm B}T(h)}, \end{equation}
where \(x_s\) is the number fraction of species \(s\), \(P\) and \(T\) are pressure and temperature, and \(k_{\mathrm B}\) is Boltzmann's constant. The line-of-sight column density through layer \(i\) is
\begin{equation} N_{s,i} = \int_{\mathcal{L}_i} n_s[h(\ell)]\,\mathrm{d}\ell, \end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{L}_i\) is the light path within the layer and \(\ell\) measures distance along it. The path is calculated from the observation airmass, supplied explicitly or read from metadata, accounting for spherical geometry and atmospheric refraction.

Absorption is evaluated on a grid finer than the detector sampling. The AER catalogue \citep{AER} supplies line positions, integrated strengths \(S_j(T_{\mathrm{ref}})\), and broadening parameters at \(T_{\mathrm{ref}}=296\,\mathrm{K}\) \citep{HITRAN}. For transition \(j\) in layer \(i\), the cross-section at wavenumber \(\tilde{\nu}=1/\lambda\), where \(\lambda\) is wavelength, is
\begin{equation} \sigma_{j,i}(\tilde{\nu}) = S_j(T_i)\,\phi_{j,i}(\tilde{\nu}). \end{equation}
Here, \(S_j(T_i)\) is the strength adjusted to the layer temperature \(T_i\), and \(\phi_{j,i}\) is the effective Voigt-based profile. Local pressure, temperature, and composition determine the line position and Doppler and collisional broadening. The discrete-line optical depth is
\begin{equation} \tau_{\mathrm{lines}}(\tilde{\nu}) = \sum_i\sum_s N_{s,i}\sum_{j\in s}\sigma_{j,i}(\tilde{\nu}), \end{equation}
where \(j\in s\) denotes transitions of species \(s\). Enabled atmospheric continuum contributions, including water-vapour and carbon-dioxide absorption, give an additional optical depth \(\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}\) \citep{MTCKD,LBLRTM}. The total optical depth and Beer--Lambert transmission are \citep{RYBICKI1979}
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tau_{\mathrm{atm}}(\tilde{\nu}) &= \tau_{\mathrm{lines}}(\tilde{\nu})+\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}(\tilde{\nu}),\\
        T_{\mathrm{atm}}(\tilde{\nu}) &= \exp[-\tau_{\mathrm{atm}}(\tilde{\nu})].
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, \(T_{\mathrm{atm}}\) is the intrinsic atmospheric transmission, before detector sampling and instrumental broadening.

\subsection{Spectral segmentation and fit-region selection}
\begin{figure*}[t]

PyMolFit divides the spectrum into processing segments while preserving spectral-order and detector-group boundaries, identified from metadata or inferred from large wavelength gaps. The default target width is 50\,\AA, with further subdivision if the atmospheric grid exceeds two million points per segment. These configurable limits control memory use without coarsening the model grid.

Fitting regions focus parameter estimation on telluric absorption, while exclusion regions remove stellar features or defective pixels that could bias the fit. Only valid pixels within fitting regions and outside exclusions contribute to the comparison between the observed spectrum and the model.

The function \texttt{select\_telluric\_regions()} automatically generates fitting windows around a requested number of AER transitions. Transitions are ranked by
\begin{equation}
    A_j = S_j(T_{\mathrm{ref}})\,N_{s(j),\mathrm{rep}},
\end{equation}
where \(S_j(T_{\mathrm{ref}})\) is the reference strength of transition \(j\), and \(N_{s(j),\mathrm{rep}}\) is the column density of its species \(s(j)\) in a fixed reference atmosphere. The highest-scoring lines within the observed coverage are selected. Each window extends a configurable number of detector pixels on either side of its line centre; the defaults are 100 lines and a half-width of 12 pixels. Lines in wavelength gaps are skipped, and overlapping windows are merged.

Enabling the interactive editor displays the generated intervals for inspection and manual adjustment (Fig.~\ref{fig:Interactive_selector}). Fitting and exclusion intervals can then be saved together in an ECSV file and supplied to the correction.

When no fitting regions are supplied to \texttt{correct()}, it selects them automatically using the same ranking score, but distributes the selected lines across wavelength and molecular species and adapts window widths to the estimated instrumental broadening.

Stellar exclusions can be generated from a theoretical stellar spectrum. PyMolFit continuum-normalises the template and broadens it for stellar rotation and, when a resolving power is supplied or available in the metadata, instrumental resolution. The template is converted to the observation's wavelength medium and reference frame using the stellar radial velocity and observation frame correction. Features exceeding a specified fractional deviation from the template continuum define exclusion intervals. These intervals are widened by a configurable velocity margin to include line wings and allow small alignment errors.

\subsection{Spectral modelling and parameter estimation}
With the fitting and exclusion regions defined, PyMolFit performs preliminary fits to the selected observed pixels to choose the wavelength-correction and instrumental-broadening models. For each spectral order or detector group, it compares the original wavelength calibration with constant, linear, and, where supported, quadratic corrections. Instrumental broadening initially uses a Gaussian profile; preliminary fits assess whether adding Lorentzian wings or wavelength-dependent widths improves the model sufficiently to justify the additional parameters. Boxcar broadening can be enabled explicitly.

The main fit uses these selected model forms. Molecular scaling factors multiply the atmospheric-profile columns to calculate the intrinsic transmission \(T_{\mathrm{atm}}\) defined above; unity leaves the columns unchanged. After applying the wavelength correction, the transmission is averaged over each observed pixel's wavelength interval, accounting for partial overlaps at pixel boundaries, and convolved with the normalised instrumental line-spread function. The resulting transmission at pixel \(p\) in segment \(k\) is denoted by \(\widehat T_{kp}\). Only the model is resampled; the observation remains unchanged.

A local polynomial continuum scales this transmission to the observed flux level:
\begin{equation}
    C_{kp} = \sum_{m=0}^{d} c_{km}u_{kp}^{m},
    \qquad
    F^{\mathrm{mod}}_{kp} = C_{kp}\,\widehat T_{kp}.
\end{equation}
Here, \(c_{km}\) is the coefficient of polynomial term \(m\) in segment \(k\), \(d\) is the continuum order, and \(u_{kp}\) maps wavelength linearly from \(-1\) to \(+1\) across the segment. This flux continuum is distinct from the atmospheric continuum absorption in \(\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}\). Automatic selection starts with \(d=2\) and compares orders 1--4 using temporarily withheld wavelength blocks, retaining the quadratic unless validation favours a simpler or better-predicting alternative.

Agreement between the modelled and observed flux inside the fitting regions is measured using uncertainty-normalised residuals. The default fit minimises the soft-\(L_1\) objective implemented in SciPy's \texttt{least\_squares} routine \citep{SCIPY}\footnote{\url{https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.least_squares.html}}:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        r_{kp}
        &= \frac{F^{\mathrm{obs}}_{kp}-F^{\mathrm{mod}}_{kp}}
                 {\sigma_{F,kp}}, \\
        \mathcal{J}
        &= a^2 \sum_k \sum_{p\in\mathcal F_k}
        \left[\sqrt{1+\left(r_{kp}/a\right)^2}-1\right].
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, \(F^{\mathrm{obs}}_{kp}\) is the observed flux, \(\sigma_{F,kp}\) its uncertainty, and \(\mathcal F_k\) contains the valid pixels inside fitting regions after exclusions. The dimensionless scale \(a\) controls the reduced influence of large residuals. It starts at unity and can be adjusted automatically between 1 and 2 using the residual scatter. Ordinary weighted least squares is also available.

PyMolFit uses the flux uncertainties supplied with the spectrum. If these are unavailable, it estimates the local noise from fluctuations between neighbouring pixels, reducing the influence of smooth continuum slopes and isolated outliers. Pixels outside the telluric fitting windows are preferred to avoid interpreting absorption features as noise. If no reliable estimate can be obtained, an uncertainty of 1\% of the local flux level is assumed.

A bounded trust-region reflective optimiser repeatedly updates the molecular scales and enabled wavelength and broadening parameters, recalculates the model, and evaluates its agreement with the observation. Molecular scales are fitted logarithmically and shared across segments, while atmospheric pressure and temperature profiles remain fixed. Wavelength corrections are shared within each physical order or detector group. At each trial, continuum coefficients are determined independently for each segment using iteratively reweighted linear least squares for robust fitting, or weighted linear least squares for ordinary fitting.

Iteration stops when the relative objective improvement, parameter step, or scaled gradient meets its convergence criterion. The default tolerances are \texttt{ftol} = \texttt{xtol} = \texttt{gtol} = \(10^{-10}\). Reaching the model-evaluation limit also stops the fit but does not establish convergence.
